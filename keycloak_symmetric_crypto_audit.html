HmacSHA1 is not quantum-vulnerable in the PQC sense, but SHA-1 has known classical weaknesses and is NIST-deprecated for new uses. The OTP secret is symmetric and short-lived — not a quantum harvest-now-decrypt-later target. The real concern is SHA-1 use more broadly.

The default realm OTP policy uses HmacSHA1 — most deployments operate with this default. Upgrading to HmacSHA256 or HmacSHA512 is advisable as a hygiene measure. Summary — Symmetric Crypto & PQC

No PQC-broken symmetric algorithms found in Keycloak production code. Symmetric algorithms (AES, HMAC, Argon2, PBKDF2) are not broken by Shor's algorithm. Grover's algorithm provides at most a quadratic speedup, effectively halving the key-length security margin. AES-256 and HS512 remain quantum-safe; AES-128 and HS256 retain ~64-bit post-quantum security which is marginal but not immediately threatened.
Two classical (non-PQC) concerns worth addressing:
  1. PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA1 (S10) — Deprecated factory retained for migration. SHA-1 has classical collision weaknesses. Credentials stored with this algorithm should be force-rehashed at next login. Not a PQC concern but a hygiene one.
  2. HOTP/TOTP default = HmacSHA1 (S12) — Default realm OTP policy uses SHA-1 per RFC 4226 compatibility. Not a quantum vulnerability (OTP secrets are not harvest-now-decrypt-later targets), but operators running security-sensitive deployments should consider migrating enrolled authenticators to HmacSHA256/512 where authenticator app support permits.
Symmetric algorithms are not the PQC risk surface in Keycloak. The entire PQC risk sits in the asymmetric layer — RSA and ECDSA/ECDH key pairs used for JWT signing and JWE key encapsulation (your existing 61-row analysis). Symmetric content encryption (AES-GCM, AES-CBC-HMAC) is safe once the asymmetric key transport layer is replaced with ML-KEM.
