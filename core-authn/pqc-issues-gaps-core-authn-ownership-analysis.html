  • The PQC programme is largely owned outside core-authn. The vast majority of issues and gaps belong to core-protocols (OIDC/SAML/JOSE), core-crypto (key management/keystores), and infra (TLS/Infinispan/containers).
  • core-authn's clear PQC slice is narrow but important: the three WebAuthn issues (#50084, #50085, #50086) and four gaps (GAP-1, GAP-22, GAP-10, and partially GAP-4/JAR) are directly within their authenticator and broker ownership.
  • GAP-1 (JWK Thumbprint for ML-DSA) is the most critical gap for core-authn — it blocks AttestationBasedClientAuthenticator (§1.1) and DPoP flows. This should be the highest-priority new issue core-authn champions.
  • GAP-22 (Attestation-Based Client Auth algorithm enforcement) is a security TODO sitting unimplemented inside a core-authn-owned authenticator — it is owned outright and has no dependency on other teams.
  • The ambiguous issues/gaps (cookies, JAR decryption, client SDK, DB schema) require cross-team coordination where core-authn should be a reviewer/stakeholder rather than the primary implementer.
